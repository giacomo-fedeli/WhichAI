# WhichAI content audit — September 8, 2026

Full feature-by-feature review, ordered by impact. Findings marked **FIXED in
v0.33** were dealt with in session 34; everything else is the queue.

The verdict in one line: the writing is better than almost anything in this
category, and the data underneath it kept falling one model generation behind
while the site's own voice ("the new #1", "right now", "days ago") turned lag
into apparent error.

---

## 1. Numbers written into prose — **FIXED in v0.33**

- **GPT-6** sat at the top of every score-sorted list with a fabricated
  `est: 65`, while its own text said OpenAI had announced nothing — and the
  Methodology page promised rumoured models "never appear in rankings".
  GPT-6 Astra had in fact shipped on September 4.
- **Eleven reviews** carried rank claims (`"#3 on the AA index (57.1)"`) that
  rot within weeks and then contradict the score rendered beside them.
- **Four modules** hardcoded "July 16 snapshot" while the database said August.
- **`benchmarks.js`** kept its own copy of six scores, already drifted, and its
  `topModel` strings recommended GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro — two generations
  behind, and Gemini 3.1 Pro is the *weakest* current Gemini in our own data.

Fixed structurally: unmeasured models carry no `score.aa` at all, ranks are
computed at render time, dates are read from `DB.updated`, and the benchmark
file derives scores via `liveScore()`. Four tests now prevent regression.

## 2. AI Debates — two are factually wrong

**The format is excellent and should be protected**: `numbers → sideA → sideB →
status → sources`, with both sides given their strongest version, is better
than most journalism manages. The `status` field forces a dated, falsifiable
claim — which is exactly why three of them are now demonstrably stale.

### `regulation` — wrong, and was already wrong when written
Says compliance deadlines "are becoming real engineering work". The EU AI Act
**Digital Omnibus** (political agreement 6 May 2026, confirmed 13 May) deferred
Annex III high-risk obligations from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**, and Annex I
to **2 Aug 2028**. Unchanged: GPAI obligations (in force since Aug 2025),
Article 5 prohibitions, Article 50 transparency. New: a prohibition on
AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery.
The `numbers` block ("Aug 2025 →", "risk tiers") contains no numbers.

### `copyright` — misses the biggest development
On **2 September 2026** the US DOJ filed in *NYT v. OpenAI* supporting the
fair-use position for model training. The executive branch taking a side is a
category change from "courts deciding case by case". The `numbers` block
("Dozens of suits", "2 reports") is filler.

### `open-vs-closed` — contradicted by our own Model Radar
Says "the gap keeps shrinking". On the September snapshot it **widened** to 6.0
points. Worse, our own `changes.js` records the decisive event on 14 August:
GLM-5.3 was the first GLM flagship to ship **without** open weights. The debate
page does not know about the scoop the site itself wrote.
Better metric available: Epoch AI measures the open/closed lag at **~4 months**
(≈8 ECI points); the UK AISI puts cyber-capability lag at 4–7 months. "Months
behind" is more intuitive than index points and is the frame experts use.

### `jobs` — right instinct, weaker numbers than exist
The `-20% for 22–25-year-old developers` figure checks out (Stanford HAI AI
Index 2026) — keep it. The headline "~16,000/month" should become the
Challenger series: **0.6% → 4.5% → 13%**, the share of US job cuts where
employers named AI, for 2024 / 2025 / 2026 YTD. And the gift: the monthly
figure recently *fell* to its lowest since December 2025. A non-monotonic trend
is exactly what the both-sides format exists to carry.

### `energy` — strongest of the six, one mislabelled figure
"~5th largest country" is labelled "on aggressive 2026 estimates". At today's
~415–500 TWh data centres rank about **11th–12th**. The 5th-place framing only
holds at the IEA's **2030** projection of 945 TWh. Change the label, keep the
claim.

### Debates that should exist
1. **"Do benchmarks measure anything?"** — the missing one, and the most
   on-brand possible. WhichAI sells benchmark-derived advice on every screen and
   has never put benchmarks on trial. Anchors it already owns: the top ten
   inside 6.3 points, and August's rebase moving the whole table 1.5–2.5 points
   — the cleanest possible demonstration that "the score went up" is not "the
   model got better". Natural home for a link into Blind Arena.
2. **"Is anyone actually more productive?"** — METR's RCT (experienced
   developers 19% *slower* with AI while believing they were 20% faster) against
   the controlled studies showing large gains for less-experienced workers. The
   self-perception gap argues directly for Compare over intuition.
3. **"Should AI answers replace the open web?"** — WhichAI is itself a site
   competing with a chatbot answer to its own question. Writing this honestly,
   including where it cuts against the site, would be the most credible page on it.
4. **"What do the labs do with your prompts?"** — the most actionable, and it
   ties to the Private-by-design badge and the BYOK architecture.

## 3. Generator — 13 families, 5 distinct prompts

The title, the OG description and the hero all promise "prompts optimized for
each of 13 AI families". Verified against `js/engine.js`: **seven families
receive a byte-identical prompt**. Tick Kimi and DeepSeek to compare and you get
the same text twice.

The `why` bullets give it away: GLM's cites GLM-5.2 as the strongest open coder
(GLM-5.3-Flash is), Kimi's cites K2.6 (K3 exists at 59.7), Grok's claims a
2M-token context (Grok 4.6 has 500K — and our own model card flags that as a
caveat), Meta's says Meta AI runs on Llama (our own DB says the Muse line).

**The most valuable fix in the file:** ship `buildReasoning`. For models where
thinking is on by default — Opus 5, Fable 5.x, GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.6 at xhigh,
DeepSeek's reasoning line — published guidance is to **omit** step-by-step
instructions, because prescribing the reasoning path degrades it. WhichAI
currently injects a mandated three-phase `<approach>` block into *every* prompt.
The flagship feature is applying anti-guidance to the most expensive models it
targets.

Then `buildDeepSeek`/`buildQwen` (real published differences: instructions in
the user turn rather than a system prompt; Qwen needs the output language stated
explicitly because it matches the *content* language) and `buildAgentic` for the
models our own DB calls agentic-first.

If a family genuinely has no distinct best practice, say so in the UI and drop
the count to the honest number. An honest 8 beats a padded 13.

## 4. Prompt Doctor — the weakest feature, and wrong in the user's favour

Measured with the real `CHECKS` array:

| Input | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Keyword-stuffed nonsense ("You are a thing. The audience is developers. Use a table… blah blah") | **100** |
| A genuinely excellent prompt (named artifact, named reader, 120-word limit, two banned words, anti-hallucination clause) | **57** |

Every check is `regex.test() → +weight`, additive, with **no penalty term**. The
tool cannot express "this is worse for having done that", which is most of what
prompt review is. It currently teaches users to pad prompts with ritual phrases.

Three fixes: add negative checks (contradictory pairs, empty intensifiers,
stacked unrelated asks, politeness padding) and cap the score when one fires;
make it task-aware (it already calls `detectTaskType()` and then ignores it);
and replace the "length ≥ 8 words" check with a **concreteness** signal — does
the prompt contain a number, a proper noun, a named artifact, a quoted string.
That one substitution reverses the ranking above.

## 5. Stack Optimizer — cannot recommend two of its own six subscriptions

`scoreApps()` initialises scores for `claude, chatgpt, gemini, perplexity` only,
but `SUBS` contains six entries including **Copilot Pro** and **SuperGrok**.
Neither exists in `APP_DB`, `FREE_TIER_NOTE` or `benchmarks.apps`.

A user who selects SuperGrok is therefore **always told to cancel it**, in every
configuration — while Grok 4.6 sits sixth overall in our own database. It prints
`redundant` with the confidence of a calculation that never ran.

The biggest missing feature is also here: **"do you need a subscription at
all?"** Every model carries `priceIn`/`priceOut` and the site never lets anyone
multiply. A moderate user at ~40 messages/week is roughly 1M input + 200K output
tokens/month, which on Gemini 3.8 Flash is about **$1.50** — a thirteenth of a
Plus subscription. No leaderboard will ever compute that for you.

Also add "what you lose" to every cancel recommendation.

## 6. The rest, briefly

- **Glossary** — contradicts the site (says GLM is open-weight; GLM-5.3 shipped
  closed) and omits the vocabulary the site itself uses: MoE, prompt caching,
  reasoning effort, tok/s, Elo, quantization, prompt injection, MCP, computer
  use, agentic. Add **"open weights ≠ open source"**, and note in the Token entry
  that non-English text costs ~1.5–2× more tokens — useful on a site in 11 languages.
- **Chains** — eight structurally identical plan/do/review triplets that
  duplicate the Generator, wasting the one thing they have that nothing else
  does: a different model per step. Bake routing into the templates.
- **Blind Arena** — the most intellectually honest feature here. Missing: seeded
  starter prompts (a blank box after "run a blind test" is a dead end), an
  interpretation of the resulting Elo, and a "your ranking vs the public
  ranking" diff, which is the payoff of the whole exercise.
- **Model Radar** — the best content on the site. Out-of-order array, and
  nothing added between Aug 30 and Sept 8 while four top-ten models shipped.
- **Merge studio** — novel and correctly framed, completely uncoached. Add three
  lines on *how* to merge, and name the failure mode: three fluent answers
  agreeing on a wrong fact still reads as consensus.
- **FAQ** — "Every major model performs measurably best with English
  instructions" is stated as fact with no source, on a site that cites
  everything else. Missing FAQs people actually arrive with: does this vendor
  train on my chats, what do I get per day on each free tier, which models are
  unavailable in my country.

## What the site does that a leaderboard cannot — defend these

1. **Free-tier and price-expiry tracking.** "Tencent Hy3's `:free` route expired
   July 21"; "Sonnet 5 intro pricing ended August 31". Nobody else tracks when
   free access dies. This is the most defensible asset on the site and it is
   buried under "More ▾".
2. **Trade-off caveats.** "Grok 4.6: the 500K context is half the field, and
   ~58 tok/s is below median." A leaderboard prints 60.9 and stops.
3. **Editorial judgment.** "GLM-5.3: the break with tradition matters more than
   the score."
4. **Operational honesty about our own defaults.** Publishing why auto-run still
   points at gemini-3.6-flash buys more trust than any accuracy claim.

Where it fails to: most Model-guide reviews *were* a leaderboard in prose (now
fixed), and the thing a leaderboard structurally cannot tell you — when a model
fails, what it refuses, what the rate limit really is at 4pm — appears nowhere.

## Missing entirely

1. A **cost calculator**. The data is already there.
2. A **privacy / data-retention matrix per provider**. The site wears a
   Private-by-design badge about its own handling and says nothing about the
   providers it sends you to, which is where the risk actually lives.
3. **Anything non-text.** 118 models, all text. `changes.js` even logs FLUX 3 as
   "outside WhichAI's text-model scope" — an explicit acknowledgement of a hole.
4. **Regional availability as a filter** (EU/UK/US differences are real).
5. **Real free-tier limits per provider per day** — the fact that determines
   whether auto-run works for you at all.
6. **A saved-prompt library.** Everything else persists; the site's primary
   output does not.
