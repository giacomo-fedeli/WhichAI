Energy, water and data centers

Can AI become more efficient while its total footprint grows? · updated 2026-09-07

Yes. A cheaper query can encourage more use, and video or long-running agents need different amounts of work from a short text answer. Global electricity totals and local water stress also measure different problems. Ask which workload, in which place, with which energy supply.

485 TWhGlobal data-center electricity in 2025, including non-AI workloads
~950 TWhGlobal data-center electricity in the IEA central projection for 2030

The concern

Concentrated demand can strain grid connections and local resources even with a modest global share. Longer reasoning, video and agent workloads can outweigh efficiency gains. A national average cannot tell a community who pays for infrastructure or whether its watershed can support a site.

The counter-view

Better hardware and software lower energy per task. New clean supply, storage and flexible scheduling can help; electricity-price effects depend on spare capacity and tariff design. These are options to assess locally, not a guarantee that growth is harmless.

The 2026 IEA outlook projects roughly doubled data-center electricity demand by 2030. Local outcomes remain conditional on siting, workload growth and energy-system choices.
